RV generator sizing

Updated 2026-11-11. Wattages are typical values from the sources listed; check your appliance label.

In an RV the rooftop air conditioner decides almost everything. Size for its start, then fit the rest around it.

Manufacturers do not agree to the watt. Champion lists a 13,500 BTU RV AC at 1,500 to 2,000 W running and 2,800 to 3,500 W starting (Champion). The label on your AC unit beats any chart.

Example: AC, fridge, lights and charging

13,500 BTU AC (1,800 / 2,800) + RV fridge (180 / 600) + 4 LED bulbs (60) + phone and laptop charging (100):

  • Running: 2,140 W
  • Biggest surge: the AC, 1,000 W above its running watts
  • Peak: 2,140 + 1,000 = 3,140 W
  • With 20% headroom: look for at least 2,600 W running and 3,200 W starting

Swap in a 15,000 BTU unit and the same list becomes about 2,900 W running and 3,700 W starting.

The microwave problem

Add a 650 W microwave (1,000 W draw per Honda) while the AC runs and you are at 3,140 W running; with 20% headroom that is 3,800 W running and 4,200 W starting. The cheaper fix is the one Honda recommends at home too: do not run them at the same time. Turn the AC off for the two minutes the microwave runs (Honda).

Two small generators instead of one big one

Some inverter generators can be paired with an identical unit to double the capacity. Honda says its EU1000i, EU2000i, EU3000 Handi and EU3000is can run in parallel with another unit of the same size, so two lighter generators can do the work of a larger one (Honda). Check that your model supports a parallel kit before counting on it.

Campground safety

CO does not care that you are camping. CPSC's rule is the same: never run a generator in an enclosed space, keep it well away from windows, doors and vents, and point the exhaust away from where people sleep (CPSC). Keep a working CO alarm in the RV. Let the generator cool before refueling (OSHA), and follow campground quiet hours.

Before you start a generator

  • Outside only. Never run a portable generator inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace or shed, even with doors and windows open. CPSC says to keep it at least 20 feet from the house, not on a porch or in a carport, with the exhaust pointed away from the home (CPSC).
  • Carbon monoxide alarms. Have working battery or battery-backup CO alarms in the house (CPSC).
  • No backfeeding. Never connect a generator to your home's wiring unless a qualified electrician has installed a transfer switch (OSHA).
