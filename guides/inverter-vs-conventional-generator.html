Quiet inverter vs conventional generator

Updated 2026-11-11. Wattages are typical values from the sources listed; check your appliance label.

Both make 120 V power from a gas engine. The difference is how the engine runs and how clean the power is, and that changes noise, fuel and what you can plug in.

How a conventional generator works

A conventional generator's engine spins the alternator at a fixed speed. Honda explains that traditional generators have to run at 3,600 RPM to make 60-hertz power, whether you are running one lamp or a full load (Honda).

How an inverter generator works

An inverter generator makes AC power, converts it to DC, then converts it back to AC through an inverter that smooths and cleans it, with a microprocessor controlling the process and the engine speed. Honda says this produces power clean enough for sensitive electronics (Honda).

Where conventional generators still fit

Home backup often needs bigger output and a 240 V outlet. Generac's GP series brochure, for example, lists a 6,500 W running / 8,125 W starting model and an 8,000 W / 10,000 W model with a 120/240 V 30 A locking outlet (L14-30R), while its 3,600 W model is 120 V only (Generac). If you are powering a 240 V well pump or connecting through a transfer switch, check that the generator you are looking at actually has the outlet and voltage you need.

How to choose without a "best" list

  1. Run your list through the calculator and write down the running and starting numbers.
  2. Note whether anything on the list needs 240 V.
  3. Decide how much noise matters where it will run: a campsite or a close neighbor versus a rural lot.
  4. Compare spec sheets on both numbers, the outlets, and run time at the load you will use.

Whatever type you pick, the safety rules do not change: outside only, 20 feet from the house, CO alarms inside, and a transfer switch for any connection to house wiring (CPSC; OSHA).

Before you start a generator

  • Outside only. Never run a portable generator inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace or shed, even with doors and windows open. CPSC says to keep it at least 20 feet from the house, not on a porch or in a carport, with the exhaust pointed away from the home (CPSC).
  • Carbon monoxide alarms. Have working battery or battery-backup CO alarms in the house (CPSC).
  • No backfeeding. Never connect a generator to your home's wiring unless a qualified electrician has installed a transfer switch (OSHA).
