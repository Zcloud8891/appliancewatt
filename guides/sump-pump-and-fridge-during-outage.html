Running a sump pump and fridge in an outage

Updated 2026-11-11. Wattages are typical values from the sources listed; check your appliance label.

The two things most people need first in a rainy outage are a sump pump that keeps the basement dry and a fridge that keeps food safe. Here is the math for running both.

The two loads

Both are motors, which matters: motors pull far more power for a few seconds as they start. Virginia Cooperative Extension notes a motor can draw about three times its running current at startup (Virginia Cooperative Extension). So the starting number, not the running number, usually decides the generator size.

The math

  1. Running: 192 + 1,050 = 1,242 W.
  2. Biggest single surge: the sump pump jumps 1,100 W when it starts (2,150 − 1,050); the fridge jumps 1,008 W. Take the larger one.
  3. Peak: 1,242 + 1,100 = 2,342 W. That is the moment the pump kicks on while the fridge is already running.
  4. With 20% headroom on the running side, look for a generator rated at least 1,500 W running and 2,400 W starting.

This follows the method Generac prints on its sizing worksheet: add the running watts of everything, then add the one highest starting load (Generac).

If your fridge is older or bigger

Generac's chart lists a refrigerator/freezer at 700 W running with 2,200 W of additional starting watts (Generac). Swap that in and the pair needs about 1,750 W running and a 3,950 W peak: with 20% headroom, 2,100 W running and 4,000 W starting. That is a different class of generator, which is why it pays to read the label on your own fridge before you shop.

Check the voltage

Honda's wattage guide marks sump pumps as 240 V (Honda). Plenty of household sump pumps plug into an ordinary 120 V outlet, so look at the pump's label or cord. If yours is 240 V, the generator needs a 240 V outlet and you will want an electrician involved.

Timing the fridge

If the generator is small, you can run the fridge in shifts. FoodSafety.gov says a closed fridge keeps food safe for up to 4 hours, and a full freezer holds a safe temperature for about 48 hours (24 if half full) with the door shut. Discard perishables that sat above 40°F for more than 2 hours (FoodSafety.gov). The sump pump is the one you cannot schedule: when water comes, it has to start.

Practical setup

  • Test before the storm: run the pump and fridge on the generator for a while on a dry day.
  • Use heavy-duty, grounded cords rated for the load, run straight from the generator (OSHA; Ready.gov).
  • Do not feed power into the house through an outlet. A transfer switch installed by a qualified electrician is the only safe way to connect to house wiring (OSHA).
  • Keep the generator outside, 20 feet from the house, exhaust pointed away, and dry (CPSC).

Before you start a generator

  • Outside only. Never run a portable generator inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace or shed, even with doors and windows open. CPSC says to keep it at least 20 feet from the house, not on a porch or in a carport, with the exhaust pointed away from the home (CPSC).
  • Carbon monoxide alarms. Have working battery or battery-backup CO alarms in the house (CPSC).
  • No backfeeding. Never connect a generator to your home's wiring unless a qualified electrician has installed a transfer switch (OSHA).
