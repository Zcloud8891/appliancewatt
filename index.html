How the math works

  1. Add up running watts for everything you want on at the same time.
  2. Find the biggest extra surge. Motors (fridges, pumps, furnace blowers, AC) pull more for a few seconds when they start. Take the one appliance whose starting watts are furthest above its running watts.
  3. Peak = total running + that one extra surge. This assumes only one motor starts at a time, which is how Generac's worksheet sizes it (Generac). Honda notes you can get by with a smaller unit by starting big loads one at a time (Honda).
  4. Recommended: running watts × (1 + headroom), rounded up to the next 100, and a starting (surge) rating at least as high as your peak. Generators list both numbers; check both.

Generator safety

  • Never run a generator indoors or in a garage. That includes basements, crawlspaces and sheds, even with doors or windows open. Opening them does not give enough ventilation to stop a deadly buildup of carbon monoxide (CPSC, Dec. 12, 2025).
  • Outside, at least 20 feet from the house. Not on a porch or in a carport. Point the exhaust away from the home and any building someone could enter, and close windows near it (CPSC, same release).
  • CO is colorless and odorless. CPSC says it can kill in minutes and that people can pass out before noticing symptoms like nausea, dizziness or weakness. If anyone feels sick, dizzy or weak, get to fresh air right away (CPSC).
  • Working CO alarms. Battery or battery-backup alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas; test them monthly (CPSC release above).
  • Use a transfer switch to power the house. Never plug a generator into a wall outlet or tie it to your panel. OSHA: attach a generator to a building's wiring only through a transfer switch installed by a qualified electrician; backfeeding can electrocute utility workers and others (OSHA fact sheet).
  • Cords and weather. Plug appliances straight into the generator with grounded (3-prong) cords rated for the load; keep the generator dry, and follow the maker's instructions for rain or snow (OSHA; CPSC).
  • Refuel cold. Shut it off and let it cool before refueling; fuel spilled on hot parts can ignite. Store fuel outside living areas (OSHA).
